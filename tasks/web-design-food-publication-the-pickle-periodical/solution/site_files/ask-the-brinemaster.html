“My jar is hissing at me. Loudly. The lid bulges, the smell is sharp, and my partner has banned it from the kitchen. Should I be excited or terrified?”
— Priya R., Toronto · 4 days ago

Excited. What you’re describing is a healthy, vigorously fermenting lacto-vegetable in stage two of its lifecycle. Hissing means CO₂ is escaping faster than your seal can release it, which is normal between days 3–7 of a kraut or kimchi ferment. The sharp smell is hydrogen sulfide and lactic acid — pungent but harmless.

That said: a bulging lid is not optional. Either “burp” the jar daily by opening it briefly to release pressure, or switch to an airlock lid that does this automatically. Bulging jars can shatter in rare cases, and nobody wants kraut grenades.

A hissing jar is a jar making its rent. Pay attention, but trust the process.

The Eternal Questions

Is my ferment safe? How do I really know?
Lacto-fermentation is among the safest food preservation methods on earth. Properly salted vegetables submerged in their own brine drop to pH 3.5–4.0 within days, an environment in which dangerous pathogens cannot survive. Trust your senses: pleasant sour = good; rotten/putrid = bad. When in doubt, throw it out.
Why does my brine cloud after a few days?
Cloudiness is the lactic acid bacteria multiplying. It’s a sign of a healthy active ferment. Not a problem.
Can I use tap water?
Only if it’s not heavily chlorinated. Otherwise let it sit uncovered for 24 hours or use filtered water.
Glass jar vs ceramic crock — which is better?
Glass is great for beginners and small batches; you can watch the magic. Ceramic crocks block UV and hold larger volumes — better for serious lacto-fermenters and aged ferments.
How do I store a ferment once it’s done?
Refrigerate. Cold slows the LAB to a near-halt. A properly fermented kraut keeps 6+ months refrigerated.
Why did my kimchi turn into liquid?
Over-fermentation, too-warm temperatures, or insufficient salt. The flavor will be intense — use it in soups or stews like a Korean cook would.
Can I add honey to a ferment for sweetness?
Honey has antimicrobial properties that can slow or halt fermentation. Use sparingly, or add after fermentation completes.
Is the white film mold? My grandmother says skim it.
She’s right. The white film is usually kahm yeast — harmless. Skim it. Genuine mold (fuzzy, green, blue, black) is rare in a submerged ferment but means starting over.
What’s the easiest first ferment for a beginner?
Sauerkraut, hands down. Two ingredients, no special equipment, ready in 2 weeks, very forgiving.
How long can a ferment really last?
Properly made and refrigerated: months to years. Some traditional Korean kimchi is aged 3+ years and prized for it.
