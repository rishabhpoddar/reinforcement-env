The Smith

I keep a fire lit.

My name is Aldous Renn. I have been a blacksmith for thirty-one years, the last seventeen of them under this roof. This page is the long version of how I got here and what I believe the work is for.

I grew up between two forges. My grandfather, Calder Renn, was a wheelwright and farrier in the Blue Ridge foothills of Virginia. By the time I knew him he was retired in name only — his shop was still attached to the house, still kept its fire banked, still smelled of coal and horse and linseed. I spent my summers there from the age of six. My job at first was to pump the bellows. It paid in iced tea and quiet praise.

My father chose not to take up the trade. He went into surveying and never regretted it, though I think he regretted not pulling me away from it in time. By thirteen I knew I wanted to forge for a living. By sixteen I was apprenticed to a French-trained smith in Asheville, Henri Beaumarie, who taught me everything I know about scrollwork, finishing, and how to read a client's silence in a meeting.

I opened Ember & Ash in 2009 in a leased garage behind a hardware store. Three years later I bought the building. In 2015 we expanded into the lot next door — that became the workshop space and what I call the bench room, where students learn. In 2022 we put up the new chimney and added a second coal forge, which let us run commission work and workshops at the same time without one bleeding into the other.

I have never advertised. The work has always brought the next piece of work. I take this both as a kind of luck and a kind of responsibility — when you have only the pieces you've made to speak for you, you have to make every one of them speak clearly.

The forge is open Wednesday through Saturday. We close on Sundays for rest and on Mondays and Tuesdays for design work, paperwork, and the kind of long projects that need a quiet shop. Visitors are welcome by appointment any day we are here. Bring your own questions.

Studio History

Notable years.

Markers from seventeen years of trying to keep a single fire lit and a small business alive.

2009

Founded

Ember & Ash opens in a 600 sq-ft leased garage on Forge Road, Kingston.

2012

Bought the Building

First commission to a museum collection — the Brooklyn Historical Society.

2015

Workshop Wing

Expansion into the lot next door adds six teaching benches and a second anvil.

2018

St. Marc Gates

Largest architectural commission — pair of 12' estate gates installed in Vermont.

2019

Marisol Joins

Resident smith Marisol Vance comes on board to lead bladesmithing programs.

2022

New Chimney

Second coal forge built. Commissions and workshops can now run in parallel.

2024

Library Railing

Hudson Free Library commissions a 38-foot stair railing — our most public piece.

2026

Year in Hand

Fourteen commissions on the schedule. Four workshops a season. Same coal fire.
