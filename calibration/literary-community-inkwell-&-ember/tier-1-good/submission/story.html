Issue 18 - Fiction

The Salt Orchard

Nadia Vale
Estimated reading time 18 minutes - 2,600 words

Speculative fiction Audio available Footnotes included

The orchard survived the winter because the frost was afraid of it. Every tree stood with a crust of salt on its branches, as if the sea had risen in the night and blessed them with a blessing no priest could name. Mara learned the orchard by touch before she learned it by sight, tracing the bark in the dark until the grooves became a grammar.

At dusk she heard the bells in the roots. They rang once for the living, twice for the remembered, and a third time only when someone was about to become a story. She stopped asking which category she belonged to. The orchard was indifferent to explanations, but generous with omens.

The house at the orchard's edge held her grandmother's books, wrapped in linen and tied with red thread. Between the pages were recipes for conserving pears, prayers for the drowned, and one recipe for staying awake while a ghost walked the hall. Mara followed the instructions exactly, though she suspected the ghost had been reading over her shoulder.

By the time the moon reached the center of the sky, the trees had begun to shine. Not with fruit, but with names.
